How the sound is made

Temperature maps
Each pixel is matched to the closest color on the NASA T2M legend, which gives a temperature from −40 to 40 °C. Warm temperatures feed a pad of two saw waves that gets louder, buzzier, brighter and faster as it heats up. Cold temperatures ring as glassy ice chimes over a bed of wind. Around 5 °C both are silent, so mild areas are the quietest.
Heat and rain maps
The original rule: red, orange and yellow count as heat and play the same buzzing pad. Blue counts as rain and falls as short drips in a shared reverb.
Top to bottom
Every row is a note of an A pentatonic scale, north high and south low.
West to east
The playhead moves across the frame and pans the sound left to right. A quiet drone keeps calm regions from going silent.
Chosen area
In area mode only the boxed region is analyzed, stretched onto the same grid.
Real frames
The built-in scenes are stylized stand-ins. Load a frame or clip from NASA’s Earth Information Center to use real data. Colors that are not on the legend, such as grey borders, labels and the grey bars around a small picture, make no sound.
