How the sound is made

Temperature maps
Each pixel is matched to the closest color on the NASA T2M legend, which gives a temperature from −40 to 40 °C. Warm temperatures feed a pad of two saw waves that gets louder, buzzier, brighter and faster as it heats up. Cold temperatures ring as glassy ice chimes over a bed of wind. Around 5 °C both are silent, so mild areas are the quietest.
Heat and rain maps
The original rule: red, orange and yellow count as heat and play the same buzzing pad. Blue counts as rain and falls as short drips in a shared reverb.
Top to bottom
Every row is a note of an A pentatonic scale, north high and south low. Land temperature and rain use the minor scale (darker, more tense). Sea temperature, snow and the red and blue rule use the major scale (brighter, more open).
West to east
The playhead moves across the frame and pans the sound left to right. A quiet drone keeps calm regions from going silent.
Chosen area
In area mode only the boxed region is analyzed, stretched onto the same grid.
Real frames
Load a frame or clip from NASA’s Earth Information Center, or any picture or video, with "Source media". Colors that are not on the legend, such as grey borders, labels and the grey bars around a small picture, make no sound.
Your own legend
"Upload a legend image" reads any color-bar picture: it finds the bar, samples its colors, and guesses whether it is a smooth gradient or a row of distinct steps. Tell it what the lowest and highest values mean and it becomes a new map type, sonified the same way.
Layer toggles
Notes mutes the row data itself (Warm or Cold); Instruments mutes a specific voice (Pad, Drops, Noise bed, Drone) however the data looks; Scene mutes everything the picture drives at once, leaving only the drone.

Why Earth sounds like this

Climate change is mostly shown as pictures, which leaves out people who cannot see them. This jukebox turns the same NASA frames into sound, so heat waves, storms and ice can be heard as well as seen. Hotter air buzzes louder and faster. Heavier rain falls thicker and brings thunder. Colder water rings quieter and slower. A heat dome sounds urgent and a polar sea sounds still, which is the point.

Using it without sight

Keys
D describes the scene in words. P plays and stops. Arrow keys move the listening box in area mode; Shift with arrows resizes it.
Sound key
Press "Hear the note scale" and "Hear the warm tone" first. They teach the two sounds you will hear in whichever map is open.
Vibration
On a phone, switch on "Vibrate" under Scene. Longer buzzes mean more heat.
